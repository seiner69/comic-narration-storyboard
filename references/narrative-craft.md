# Narrative craft for source-grounded comic narration

Read when composing or revising prose. These are choices, not a checklist to insert into every passage. A novel-like narrator may select, compress, linger and interpret supported emotion; the narrator may not invent the underlying story.

## Work at the level of a conflict

Understand the complete in-scope exchange before polishing sentences: what each person asks for, what response they receive, what changes, and why the next scene follows. Read dialogue even when it will be excluded from displayed crops. Keep indispensable background; avoid both abstract explanation and unexplained action lists.

Draft natural paragraphs with unequal emphasis. An action chain tests whether the story connects; it is not a rule that each sentence must supply a fresh action. Narration may linger on a decisive look or line without adding an event. Ordinary processes can be summarized. Lines, paragraphs and visuals need not have a fixed ratio.

Keep the diagnosis behind the prose. A concrete request followed by a refusal may already make the cause clear. Explain where understanding would otherwise fail; do not label every sentence as pressure, choice or consequence.

For a fresh rewrite, return to the in-scope source before choosing the passage's shape. For targeted feedback on a confirmed draft, recheck relevant evidence and retain what works. Neither starting over nor keeping the previous wording is a universal requirement.

## Choose a technique for a particular effect

| Choice | Useful application | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contrast / different perceptions | The same delay can mean relief to one person and danger to another. | Establish each reading from the source; do not invent a hidden wish. |
| Close psychological narration | Paraphrase a stated hope, grievance or fear in the narrator's voice. | A generic expression does not establish a precise plan, memory or motive. Keep beliefs distinct from narrator-certified facts. |
| Figurative language | A reaction can land like a blow; silence can make a disagreement palpable. | Keep it recognizably nonliteral, not a new injury, ability, duration or numerical feat. |
| Summary versus scene | Compress routine transitions; retain dialogue where a relationship or decision changes. | Do not remove necessary causes for speed or visual matching. |
| Callback / meaningful repetition | An earlier assurance changes meaning after an established setback. | Repetition must change perspective or anticipation, not restate the same emotion. A verbal callback does not require replaying an image. |
| Suspense / flashback | Move an established explanation to where the listener wants it. | Stay within source range, spoiler contract and any chronological-only request; never invent a secret. |
| Information advantage / irony | A known trap changes how the audience hears an opponent's confidence. | Do not relabel genuine uncertainty as a deliberate strategy. |
| Humor / connected imagery | A prop or performance can sustain a comic comparison. | Match the actual tone; do not import comedy into an unrelated scene just because a reference used it. |

Use fitting choices without a quota of metaphors, emotional peaks or reversals. Third person is a useful default, not a reason to forbid a requested first-person adaptation. Inner perspective is not permission to invent quoted speech.

## Make viewpoint clear

An untagged rhetorical question can turn a character's grievance into the narrator's verdict. Attribute the belief where ambiguity occurs, or use a corresponding verified first-person line. Do not add disclaimers everywhere or invent dialogue solely to solve attribution.

Requests, accusations and reports are not completed outcomes. Preserve who made a claim and whether it remains contested. Stay close to the focal character without becoming their advocate: an uncomfortable grievance must not be replaced with a more flattering motive.

## Cut stagnation, not development

- Repeated descriptions of the same hesitation may hold the story still. Keep the clearest occurrence and any response that changes the situation.
- Contrasting behavior and speech can add tension without an explanatory label.
- A recalled statement after a changed relationship can acquire new meaning; it is not automatically redundant.
- Delete a bridge when the next exchange already establishes the transition. Do not impose a ban on connective phrases.
- A brief reaction can matter without adding a plot fact. Test whether removing it changes comprehension, anticipation or feeling.

When compressing prose, remove obsolete visual replays and directions. Continuous use of one panel across a paragraph break remains a hold, not a mandatory cut.

## Reference transfer

Use the requested sample size: one named excerpt for that kind of iteration, several openings only for a requested comparison. Identify the inspected passage and the actual technique being transferred. Do not stop at labels such as "fast pacing" or copy signature sentences.

An ASR filename saying "proofread" does not establish accuracy. Check suspect phrases against available audio/captions and disclose what was inspected. Do not guess missing manga facts or claim that a reference author invented a detail when its original source is unavailable.

Transfer narrative function, not another character's psychology. An apparent retreat might be a test in one story and a genuine decision in another; verify rather than importing the motive.

## Two evidence checks

1. **Factual support:** authoritative art, dialogue, narration and context support the claim. Accusations remain accusations; requests do not become outcomes. Put unresolved names, pronouns or motives outside the voiceover instead of filling them in.
2. **Visual presentation:** literal actions show the correct actor/action/result; dialogue or interpretation uses the relevant speaker, listener or state. Cropping away a bubble does not erase its factual information. An unrelated face cannot stand in for a specific action.

Ask whether a flourish changes how an established event is felt or adds a new event. Keep the former when useful; remove or verify the latter. Interpretation does not need to be OCR-matched.

## Independent fictional example

Assumed evidence: a lighthouse's main lamp goes dark. The keeper places a small backup lamp in the window and tells an apprentice that it still works. No source establishes whether ships have seen it.

- Flat recap: "主灯灭了。守灯人放上备用灯，说它还能用。"
- Permissible treatment: "整座灯塔暗了下去，窗口却还留着一点光。守灯人把备用灯推到窗前：它还能用。"
- Unsupported addition: "所有船都看见了信号，立刻避开了暗礁。"

Contrast changes the emphasis of known evidence; the last version invents an unseen result. A wide shot and a lamp detail can carry the passage without one picture per clause. This invented example is neither production material nor a universal opening.

## Final prose pass

Read without images: credible content → understandable structure → useful pacing → speakable language → visual presentation.

- Can a new listener follow relationships and consequential responses?
- Are fact, accusation, demand, belief and interpretation distinct?
- Does the opening establish tension that the passage develops?
- Is an unknown being invented merely to close a causal gap?
- Where does expectation change, and where is the same state only restated?
- Whose judgment is heard, and which detail carries the feeling?
- Are pronouns and transitions clear when spoken?
- Does the art carry the relevant action, speaker or reaction while narration supplies necessary meaning?

Then check mapped art and source dialogue. Finalize narration in the storyboard and derive the delivered script from it. See [gold-standard.md](gold-standard.md) for the limited purpose of synthetic fixtures.

Do not assign a self-certified "viral" score or promote a draft to an approved baseline without the user's decision. Describe concrete choices and unresolved facts.
